# Buenos Aires: places from the books

Places in Buenos Aires that your Argentina books mention, or that relate to them, with the best time of day and week to go. Claude made this list on 25 November 2026 by searching inside eight of the ten Argentina books and going through your notes and chapter 1. Hedges and Gilliland aren't online, so for those it used your notes and the web. Opening times are from official sites where possible; "check" marks the ones where sources were old or disagreed. It assumes you are here until about Christmas.

Where you have already been is in [diary.md](diary.md).

## Your week

- **Monday:** most museums are closed. Do the cemeteries, the Parque de la Memoria and pizza on Corrientes.
- **Wednesday:** ESMA in the morning, the Abuelas' house at 15:30, the Parque de la Memoria at sunset. The gaucho museum is free on Wednesdays.
- **Thursday:** the Tierra del Fuego museum and the old bookshop from 13:00, the Mothers at 15:30, a milonga after midnight.
- **Saturday:** the Chacarita tour, polo, and Sunderland at night.
- **Sunday:** San Telmo, or Mataderos. You have four Sundays, so you can do both.

## The dictatorship (France, Timerman, Gilliland)

- **The Mothers' march, Plaza de Mayo.** France watched them gather "every Thursday afternoon, as they had done since 1977". They still march, around the pyramid, where their white headscarves are painted on the ground. **When:** Thursdays at 15:30.
- **ESMA** (Av. del Libertador 8151). This is the Navy Mechanics School, France's "most terrible concentration camp of all", now a museum. Babies born to prisoners here were taken and given away, the crime at the centre of Gilliland's book. France met a survivor who had seen lists pinned to a wall, on which military staff noted "the characteristics they were looking for in an adoptive baby". On the same grounds, the Abuelas run the Casa por la Identidad. **When:** 10:00–17:00. The national site says Tuesday to Sunday and the Espacio Memoria site says Wednesday to Saturday, so Saturday is safe. Arrive at 10:00, allow 2–3 hours, and take the English audioguide. On Saturdays there are guided visits in Spanish at 11:00 and 14:00, with no booking. The Casa por la Identidad lists guided visits on Wednesdays at 15:30 and Thursdays at 11:00, but the listing is old, so check.
- **Parque de la Memoria** (Costanera Norte 6745). A wall of names of the disappeared stands on the shore of the river where, as France writes, the death flights dropped them: she cites the confession of Adolfo Scilingo, a navy officer, to throwing drugged prisoners out of planes on night flights. **When:** daily, 10:00–18:00; its exhibition hall, the Sala PAyS, opens at 11:00. Go in the late afternoon, after ESMA, which is on the same side of town.

## Cemeteries (France, Hedges, Bridges)

- **Recoleta, then La Biela.** France was shown round by Hugo, a grave-tender for thirty years. Evita lies in the Duarte vault, "about five metres under"; Rosas and his "sworn enemy" Sarmiento are here too. Across the road is La Biela, France's "most fashionable cafe in Buenos Aires", which was "bombed regularly" during the Dirty War. **When:** 9:00 on a weekday, when it opens to visitors (9:00–17:00), before the heat and the tour groups. Foreigners pay about ARS 24,000, by card only. Then coffee on La Biela's terrace.
- **Chacarita, and the British Cemetery next door** (Elcano 4568). France found Gardel's life-size statue "never without flowers". Thomas Bridges died in Buenos Aires in 1898 and is buried in the British Cemetery. Lucas, who wrote *Uttermost Part of the Earth* here in 1947, is buried next to him. Perón's tomb, which France also saw, was moved out of Chacarita in 2006. **When:** daily, 8:00–17:00, best in the morning. For Gardel, go on Fri 11 Dec, his birthday. For the free tour, it's 10:00 on the 2nd or 4th Saturday. For the Bridges grave, ask at the British Cemetery office.

## Tango (Winter, France)

- **Niño Bien closed in 2013**, sadly. These are the other milongas in Winter's book:
  - **Salón Canning** (Scalabrini Ortiz 1331): its milonga used to run on Monday, Tuesday and Friday from about 22:30.
  - **La Viruta** (Armenia 1366): a class first, then dancing until dawn at weekends.
  - **Sunderland** (Lugones 3100 block, Villa Urquiza): the "purest" milonga, in an old basketball gym, on Saturday nights.
  - **Sin Rumbo** (Villa Urquiza): a famous club, founded in 1919.

  Their websites are blank or down, so check the night in the Hoy Milonga app before you go. **When:** Winter went on Thursdays and Saturdays and planned to arrive "early, around 1" in the morning. Watching from a table is fine.
- **La Ideal** (Suipacha 384). France spent a winter Friday afternoon here, and Winter was the only foreigner there. It reopened as a café in 2022; whether it still holds milongas wasn't confirmed. **When:** a weekday afternoon, 16:00–19:00.
- **Plaza Dorrego, San Telmo.** A free open-air milonga after the Sunday market, and a gentle first look. **When:** Sundays, about 18:00–21:00.

## 2001 and Avenida de Mayo (Winter, Mount)

- **Walk Avenida de Mayo from the Plaza to Congress.** Plaques mark where people were killed on 20 December 2001: Gastón Riva at Tacuarí, Gustavo Benedetto at Chacabuco. Have lunch on the way at **Pedemonte** (no. 676), Mount's "Buenos Aires landmark", founded in 1890 and famous for its artichoke tart. A Malbec there counts as Mendoza homework. **When:** for the anniversary, the evening of Sat 19 Dec (the pot-banging began that night, after De la Rúa's speech) and Sun 20 Dec (families visit the plaques). Otherwise go on a weekday, because the centre empties at weekends.

## Pizza and the Italians (your chapter, Winter)

- **Güerrín** (Corrientes 1368, since 1932). This is the Buenos Aires pizza your chapter breaks off at: thick, baked in a pan, eaten standing at the counter with a slice of fainá (Genoese chickpea bread) on top. Go on a night walk down Corrientes, Winter's "Broadway", past "the great bookshops" he browsed. **When:** any night, 22:00 to midnight. It's open 11:00–1:00, until 2:00 on Friday and Saturday, and the queues are worst at lunch and around 21:00.
- **Ñoquis del 29.** An Italian-immigrant custom: gnocchi on the 29th of the month, with a banknote under the plate for luck. **When:** dinner on Sun 29 Nov or Tue 29 Dec.
- **Museo de la Inmigración**, in the 1911 Hotel de Inmigrantes, where new arrivals were put up free. **When:** Wednesday to Sunday, 11:00–18:00, free. There's a weekend guided tour at 15:00.

## Gauchos (Guerriero, Hudson)

- **Feria de Mataderos** (Lisandro de la Torre and Av. de los Corrales). There's folk dancing in the street, gaucho riding games, locro and empanadas, and the small Museo Criollo de los Corrales. The barrio is named after the slaughterhouses that replaced Hudson's south-side "killing-grounds". Someone in Guerriero's book lives here and calls it "a very unfortunate name". It's your best chance of seeing malambo: Laborde is in January, and no Buenos Aires qualifier turned up. **When:** Sundays, 11:00–19:00, until the end of December, except in rain. It's an hour west, so arrive around 12:00–13:00 for lunch and stay for the afternoon dancing.
- **Museo de Arte Popular José Hernández** (Av. del Libertador 2373). Gaucho silver, mate gourds and ponchos. It's named after the author of *Martín Fierro*, which Guerriero's dancers read. **When:** Tuesday to Friday 13:00–19:00, weekends 10:00–20:00, free on Wednesdays.
- **La Morena** (Austria 2032, between Juncal and French). A restaurant of northern food that turns into a peña every night: from about 23:00, regulars and guests play guitar and sing at the tables. They play chacareras, zambas and cuecas, and Guerriero's dancers dance the zamba and the cueca at Laborde. Order empanadas salteñas, humita or a tamal. **When:** any night from about 22:00. The singing gets going after midnight, and from Thursday to Saturday it runs late. Book on 11 6016-9415.

## Tierra del Fuego (Bridges, your chapter)

- **Museo Etnográfico** (Moreno 350). Its exhibition "The uttermost part of the world" covers the European occupation of Tierra del Fuego and what happened to the Selk'nam and Yaghan. **When:** Tuesday to Friday 13:00–19:00, Saturday 15:00–19:00.
- **Librería de Ávila** (Alsina 500), the city's oldest bookshop, once called the Librería del Colegio. Bridges thanks it for the Ona photographs in his book. They came from Gallardo's *Los Onas*, which it published in 1910. **When:** a weekday, during the day.
- **The Roca monument** (Diagonal Sur). Bridges describes the bronze general on "a mighty stallion", with just "ROCA" on the marble. Roca was the president who gave Bridges's father the land for his farm, Harberton. **When:** any time.
- All three are a few blocks from the Plaza. Do them on a Thursday from 13:00 and finish at the Mothers' march at 15:30.

## Palermo's park (Hudson, Bridges, Winter)

- **Rodin's statue of Sarmiento** (Libertador and Sarmiento) stands on the site of the "white stuccoed house" of Hudson's "Tiger of Palermo", Rosas. A 1999 statue of Rosas almost faces it. The park is called Tres de Febrero after the date in 1852 when Rosas was beaten, in the battle whose guns Hudson heard as a boy. Bridges's Ona party camped in this park for an exhibition, and the rich of Winter's history drove their carriages through it. The **Ecoparque** nearby is the old zoo, where Bridges argued with its director about guanacos. **When:** a weekday late afternoon. The Ecoparque is open Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00–18:00; visitors from outside the city now pay. The **Museo Evita** (Tuesday to Sunday, about 11:00–19:00) is close by, if you want more Evita after Hedges.

## Animals and horses (Durrell, Hudson, your chapter)

- **Reserva Ecológica Costanera Sur.** River birds, coypu and turtles. Hudson's bien-te-veo, the bird that calls out its own name ("I can see you"), is everywhere. **When:** Tuesday to Sunday, about 8:00–19:00 in summer, closed when it rains. Go at opening, when the birds are busiest and before the heat.
- **Museo Argentino de Ciencias Naturales** (Ángel Gallardo 490). Durrell knew "the people in charge of the Museum at Buenos Aires" and kept his animals in a shed in its grounds, probably this one. **When:** a weekday afternoon, 14:00–19:00.
- **Polo: the Argentine Open in Palermo.** France's rich "attended polo matches at the Hurlingham". The ponies are bred on estancias like Durrell's and Hudson's, which makes them the end of your chapter's horse story. **When:** the final is expected on the afternoon of Sat 12 Dec (check). Earlier rounds are on weekend afternoons.

## Day trip (Hudson)

- **Hudson's birthplace** in Florencio Varela, about 35 km south. It's now a museum at Los Veinticinco Ombúes, the house "of the twenty-five ombú trees" where *Far Away and Long Ago* begins. **When:** Saturday or Sunday, 11:00–17:00. Those are 2022 hours, so check its Instagram, @museohudsonpba.

## One evening: a night on Corrientes

Claude picked this for Thursday 26 November, but it works on any night. It puts the pizza your chapter stops at together with places from Winter's and France's books, all open late.

- **18:30, La Ideal** (Suipacha 384, at the corner of Corrientes). Have a coffee or a vermouth; it's open until 1:00.
- **19:30, the bookshops.** Walk west along Corrientes, across 9 de Julio by the Obelisco. The stretch between Esmeralda and Callao is "the great bookshops of Avenida Corrientes" that Winter browsed, and they stay open well after dark.
- **22:00, Güerrín** (Corrientes 1368). Order at the counter and eat standing up: a slice of muzzarella with fainá on top. By 22:00 the 21:00 rush has eased.
- **Before,** on a Thursday: the Mothers' march in Plaza de Mayo at 15:30. From there La Ideal is about 15 minutes' walk up Diagonal Norte.
- **After:** El Beso (Riobamba 416, a few blocks further along Corrientes) has a milonga most nights; check its Instagram (@elbesotango) first. Winter danced on Thursdays and arrived around 1 in the morning.

## Eva Perón (Hedges, France, Winter, Mount)

Added on 27 November 2026, when you said you'd like to see more of Evita. In the order of her story:

- **Luna Park** (Corrientes and Bouchard). In the usual telling, she met Perón here on 22 January 1944, at a benefit for the victims of the San Juan earthquake. Some historians put their first meeting a few days earlier. It has been closed since the end of 2024, and in April 2026 a court halted the work to rebuild it. **When:** any time, from outside.
- **Museo Evita** (Lafinur 2988). Until the 1955 coup the house was a shelter run by her foundation for single mothers, and for widowed or separated women with children. France and Mount both write about the foundation. The museum's restaurant is in the same house, with tables in the patio, and its entrance is round the back, at Juan María Gutiérrez 3926. It stays open after the museum closes: in December 2025 it was open every day until midnight. **When:** the museum, Tuesday to Sunday, 11:00–19:00.
- **The steel Evitas on 9 de Julio** (between Moreno and Belgrano; Line C to Moreno). On 22 August 1951 she spoke from a stage here, in front of what was then the Ministry of Public Works, to a huge crowd that wanted her to run for vice-president. Nine days later she turned it down on the radio. Since 2011 the building has carried two giant steel portraits of her, a smiling one at one end and a fierce one at a microphone at the other. Since 2024 the government has talked of demolishing the building, and in October 2026 a politician proposed swapping one of the portraits for a giant Messi, so don't leave it too late. **When:** in daylight. They were lit at night until March 2024, when the government switched the lights off.
- **Plaza Evita and the Biblioteca Nacional** (Libertador and Agüero). She died here on 26 July 1952, in the presidential residence, the Palacio Unzué. The military government that overthrew Perón in 1955 knocked it down, and the National Library was later built on the site. A statue of her by Ricardo Gianetti has stood in the plaza since 1999. **When:** any time. It's ten minutes' walk from Recoleta cemetery.
- **The CGT** (Azopardo 802). Her embalmed body lay on the second floor of the trade unions' headquarters from 1952 until an army commando took it in November 1955. France describes an office block, near a junction I couldn't make out, where it was then "hidden in an attic" before it was smuggled out of the country in 1957. The rooms where it lay, once her office, are now a small museum, but I couldn't find when it opens. Since 2023 the building has been on Argentina's tentative list for UNESCO World Heritage status. Across the road, at Paseo Colón 850, is the columned building her foundation put up as its headquarters. She died before she could use it, and after the 1955 coup it was given to the university's engineering faculty. **When:** in daylight or at dusk, from outside. It's ten minutes' walk south of Plaza de Mayo.
- **Recoleta cemetery.** She lies in the Duarte family vault, "about five metres under" (France). See Cemeteries, above.
- **Day trip: the República de los Niños** (Manuel B. Gonnet, near La Plata). A child-sized city from the Peronist years, which France says was "regularly claimed as the inspiration for Disneyland". **When:** the park is open daily and free; its buildings open roughly 9:00–18:00, so check.
- **The film.** In Winter's book, a call for tango dancers turns out to be a search for extras for *Evita* (1996), in which Madonna sang from the real Casa Rosada balcony. France's chapter "Evita Aflame" covers the fuss when Hollywood came: graffiti saying "Evita is sacred" and "Evita belongs to the workers".

Two outings cover most of it. Downtown, in daylight: the steel Evitas, then the CGT and the foundation's headquarters, about 20 minutes' walk east, and Luna Park, 20 minutes north of them. Dinner at the Museo Evita's restaurant makes a good end to it. The north side in a morning: Recoleta at 9:00, Plaza Evita, then the Museo Evita from 11:00.
