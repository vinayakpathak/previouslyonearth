# Recommendations

Books Claude has recommended, oldest first. They're kept so that no book is
suggested twice, and so that it's clear which suggestions worked: when a
recommended book is read, its line here says how it went, and its full notes
are in `books.yml`.

## 2026-10-28: a book about India

- *City of Djinns: A Year in Delhi*, by William Dalrymple (1993). The pick:
  travel memoir with Delhi's history peeled back era by era, and very funny.
  → Already read in 2025, before this record began: one of the user's
  favourites, though most of its history has since been forgotten.
- *Midnight's Furies: The Deadly Legacy of India's Partition*, by Nisid Hajari
  (2015). In reserve: a page-turner on Partition, told through a few leaders.
- *Behind the Beautiful Forevers: Life, Death, and Hope in a Mumbai Undercity*,
  by Katherine Boo (2012). In reserve: a few families in a Mumbai slum,
  reported over years.

## 2026-10-29: books about Panama

- *Panama Fever*, by Matthew Parker (2007). The pick: the building of the
  canal, told through the people who built it, including the West Indian
  workers' own letters and diaries.
- *Panama: The Whole Story*, by Kevin Buckley (1991). The dictatorship
  thread from Argentina: Noriega's fall and the 1989 US invasion, told like a
  thriller.
- *The Darkest Jungle: The True Story of the Darien Expedition and America's
  Ill-Fated Race to Connect the Seas*, by Todd Balf (2003). A survival story
  from the hunt for a canal route, half a century before the canal.

## 2026-11-04: books about Rome

- *Rubicon: The Last Years of the Roman Republic*, by Tom Holland (2003). The
  pick: how the Republic fell to strongmen, from Marius and Sulla to Caesar
  and Augustus, told as a gripping narrative. The dictatorship thread from
  Argentina, at its origin.
- *A Day in the Life of Ancient Rome*, by Alberto Angela, translated by Gregory
  Conti (2009). The light companion: one day in AD 115, hour by hour, all
  everyday life and customs, with almost no names.
- *A Traveller in Rome*, by H. V. Morton (1957). Travel writing, the form that
  has worked every time: Morton walks 1950s Rome and tells its history, from
  the Forum to the Pope at his window.

## 2026-11-06: books about Vatican City

- *The Kidnapping of Edgardo Mortara*, by David I. Kertzer (1997). The pick:
  in 1858 the Pope's police take a six-year-old from his Jewish family to be
  raised Catholic, and the outcry helps end the popes' kingdom. Gilliland's
  stolen children, a century earlier.
- *The Vatican Diaries*, by John Thavis (2013). The light companion: a
  reporter's thirty years on the Vatican beat, in funny, self-contained
  episodes, ending with Benedict XVI.
- *The Pope and Mussolini: The Secret History of Pius XI and the Rise of
  Fascism in Europe*, by David I. Kertzer (2014). The stretch: the 1929 deal
  with Mussolini that made Vatican City, and the dictatorship thread again.
  Best read after Mortara.
